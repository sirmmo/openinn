The Gilded Mimic

You keep an inn on the third level of the dungeon. Adventurers, dwarf miners, wizards, card sharps, goblin peddlers, pilgrims and delvers back from the deep come down the stairs at night: thirsty, hungry, tired, grimy, wounded or bored.

  1. By day, dig and furnish. Dig rooms next to the inn, cell by cell, then put furniture in them. Everything costs gold. You can lay a different floor in a cell (its finish), turn furniture, and rename the inn.
  2. By night, the doors are open. Guests walk to the nearest place that serves their most pressing need, pay and are served. A guest whose need nobody serves waits, sulks and leaves angry, so build for the guests who are coming.
  3. Production. The kegs tap ale, the hearth cooks stew from the larder's provisions, the boiler heats water for the tubs, mushroom beds grow where it is damp, and the copper still turns mushrooms into tonic for the bar. All of it costs a little gold.
  4. Mind the rooms. Noise, heat, dirt, cheer and damp spread from cell to cell (see Show). Wizards hate noise and damp; everyone hates dirt; most guests like cheer, which candles, banners, trophies and a contented mimic give off.
  5. Reviews. Happy guests raise your reputation, which brings more guests and new kinds of them: card sharps at 8, dwarves at 10 (grimy), delvers at 15 (wounded and grimy), wizards at 25 (bored), pilgrims at 30.
  6. Walls, doors and levels. Guests walk round inner walls, and noise and the rest stop at them; a door lets both through. Stairs down from the hall or the cellar open the level below ([ and ] switch levels): a quiet place for the map room.

Rooms

  • Hall, Taproom, Kitchen, Cellar, Dormitory: drink, food, stores and beds.
  • Shrine: an altar blesses the wounded; candle stands and pews.
  • Bathhouse: copper tubs and a boiler for grime (a washstand in the hall or the dormitory does a quick rinse).
  • Gaming den: dice and card tables for the bored. Loud.
  • Map room: bookcases, reading desks and, with surveyors' maps, a map table. Wants quiet.
  • Mushroom garden: mushroom beds and a dripping cistern, for the still.
  • Trophy hall (opened in the ledger): a coin hoard, trophies and the tame mimic.

Combos

Some furniture works better together (a unit's combos show when you point at it). Distances count diagonals, on the same level.

  • Snug corner: a table within 2 cells of the hearth. Stew dearer, guests happier.
  • Regulars' row: bar stools beside the bar. Faster drinks.
  • Under the banner: a table beside a guild banner.
  • Candles round the altar: two candle stands beside it. Deeper blessings, cheer, faith.
  • Basin by the altar: a washstand beside it.
  • Full pews: two pews beside the altar, once the ledger has a tithe box.
  • A blessed house: any altar, once the ledger's house is blessed.
  • Steam room: a tub beside the boiler, where the heat is 3 or more.
  • Hushed corner: a reading desk with two bookcases beside it, noise under 3.
  • Dice in the round: two dice tables side by side. Dearer, merrier, louder.
  • High stakes: card tables, once reputation is 40.
  • Trophies by the map: a trophy stand beside the map table.
  • The Gilded Mimic: a tame mimic within 2 cells of the bar. Guests tip to watch it.
  • Sleeping on the hoard: the mimic beside a coin hoard.
  • Fairy ring: three mushroom beds together keep their own damp.
  • Hush in the dormitory: a feather bed where the noise is under 2.

The ledger

The ledger (its tab next to Inn) runs on through the day and while you are away (up to eight hours). Peddlers, bards, foragers, scribes, acolytes, stair guides, a fence and caravans work for the inn, paid from the same purse. Guests feed it: renown from happy guests, lore from reading and maps, faith from blessings. Its improvements raise prices, bring more guests, keep them waiting longer, open the trophy hall, the map table and the mimic, and make combos stronger. Renown can't run ahead of the inn: it holds 50, plus 2 for every guest served this run. Sell the inn for legend (prices and the ledger rise with it) and start over grander: of each kind of hired people, two follow you, and one more for every legend. Much later, give up your legend for a guild's charter.

A season is seven nights. The game saves itself as you play. In the menu: Play endless keeps the inn open night after night, with more room and five levels to dig; Build from a Dwellings house starts an inn in a house made with watabou's Dwellings (its "Export as JSON"), and Export saves yours in the same format.
