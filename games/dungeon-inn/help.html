The Gilded Mimic

You keep an inn on the third level of the dungeon. Adventurers, dwarf miners, wizards and goblin peddlers come down the stairs at night, thirsty, hungry and tired.

  1. By day, dig and furnish. Dig rooms next to the inn, cell by cell, then put furniture in them: a bar and tables in the taproom, a hearth and a larder in the kitchen, kegs in the cellar, bunks and beds in the dormitory. Everything costs gold.
  2. By night, the doors are open. Guests walk to whatever serves their most pressing need, pay and are served. The kegs tap ale and the hearth cooks stew from your stores, all night, as long as there is gold to pay for them.
  3. Mind the rooms. Noise, heat and dirt spread from room to room (see Show). Wizards hate noise; everyone hates dirt; a washtub's scullion scrubs the floors around it.
  4. Reviews. Happy guests raise your reputation, which brings more guests, and pickier ones: dwarves at 10, wizards at 25. Guests who wait too long leave angry.
  5. Walls and doors. Wall rooms off from each other: guests walk round walls, and noise, heat and dirt stop at them. A door lets both through. Wall the dormitory off from the taproom and the wizards sleep better.
  6. Dig deeper. Stairs down from the hall or the cellar open the level below (use the arrows at the bottom, or [ and ], to switch levels).

The ledger (its tab next to Inn) runs on through the day and while you are away. Peddlers sell your stew on the stairs, bards sing of the inn, a fence buys loot: hire them with the same money as the inn. Its improvements raise prices, bring more guests, keep them waiting longer and unlock new furniture; sell up when it pays, and start over grander.

A season is seven nights. The game saves itself as you play. In the menu: Play endless keeps the inn open night after night, with more room and five levels to dig; Build from a Dwellings house starts an inn in a house made with watabou's Dwellings (its "Export as JSON"), and Export saves yours in the same format.
