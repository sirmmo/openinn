The Slow Orbit

You run the bar and bunkhouse on a refuelling ring at the edge of the Tessel mining belt. At station night the airlock opens, and freighter pilots, belt miners, courier robots, tourists, hull salvagers, drift pilgrims and the odd envoy come aboard: thirsty, hungry, tired, running flat, bored stiff, gritty with belt dust or carrying a rad dose home.

  1. On the dock shift, build and refit. Build out the ring next to what is there, cell by cell, then fit it out. Everything costs credits. Hover over a room or a piece of furniture to see what it does; lay a different floor in any room, turn furniture, and rename the bar.
  2. At station night, the airlock is open. Guests head for whatever serves their most pressing need, pay and are served. Tanks brew fizz, printers print meals from your rations, racks grow greens, synthesisers make med gel and reclaimers wring out water all night, as long as there are credits to pay for them.
  3. Keep the air clean and the ring homely. Everyone who breathes adds CO2, and it hardly leaks away on its own (see Show): air scrubbers clear it, and hydroponic racks and fern planters drink a little. Planters, glow lamps, starfield screens and the drift shrine give off comfort, which tourists, envoys and pilgrims like. Envoys and pilgrims hate noise.
  4. Charge the robots. Courier robots don't eat or drink, but they need a charging post, and they leave a bad review if they can't find one.
  5. Ratings. Happy guests raise your rating, which brings more ships, and different ones: belt miners at 10, tourists at 15, hull salvagers at 20, envoys at 25, drift pilgrims at 40. Guests who wait too long for what they need leave angry: have a med bay ready for the miners' rad dose, a wash deck for their grime, and something for the tourists' boredom.
  6. Bulkheads and hatches. Seal rooms off from each other: guests go round bulkheads, and noise and stale air stop at them. A hatch lets both through. Seal the cabins off from the lounge and the sim den, and the envoys sleep better, but every sealed room needs its own scrubber.
  7. Build up. A stairwell from the airlock deck or the lounge opens the deck above (use the arrows at the bottom, or [ and ], to switch decks).

Rooms

  • Airlock deck: charging posts and scrubbers. Lounge: drinks counters, mess tables, booths, recliners. Galley and Cargo hold: printers, cold stores, fizz tanks, the stores. Cabins: sleep pods and berths.
  • Hydroponics bay: hydroponic racks grow greens and eat CO2; while there are greens, mess tables, booths and greens counters serve fresh bowls, dearer and more filling than printed meals.
  • Med bay: med pods flush a rad dose out, for med gel from a gel synthesiser.
  • Observation lounge: viewport benches (cheap, slow) and star-chart tables, for the bored. Guests trade you star charts for them.
  • Sim den: game consoles (quick, loud), and sim rigs once you hold a licence.
  • Wash deck: sonic showers, and steam tubs (with steam plumbing and a water reclaimer).
  • Chapel of the drift (once you know the rite): the drift shrine, where pilgrims keep vigil and leave owing you a favour.

Combos

Some furniture works better together (on the same deck; diagonals count). Each piece's description says what it goes with.

  • Lit counter: a starfield screen beside a drinks counter (15% dearer). Long bar: two counters side by side (faster). Mess hall: three mess tables together (faster, louder).
  • Garden table: a table, booth or greens counter within two cells of a hydroponic rack (dearer, happier). Lamplit snug: a glow lamp beside a booth (a quarter dearer). Fresh-air lounge: two planters within two cells of a counter, table or booth, while the CO2 stays under 4.
  • Quiet berth: a pod or berth where the noise stays under 2 (more rest). Homely cabin: a pod, berth or recliner beside a planter or a glow lamp.
  • Window seat: a viewport bench within two cells of a star-chart table (half as dear again). Arcade row: three game consoles in a row (dearer, very loud). Sim league: two sim rigs and a starfield screen, at a rating of 40 (needs the league charter).
  • Triage bay: a med pod beside a gel synthesiser (faster, deeper). Fern steam: a shower or steam tub beside a planter. Lamplit vigil: a drift shrine with two glow lamps beside it. Charging bay: two charging posts side by side (faster).

The contracts

The contracts (their tab next to Inn) run on through the dock shift and while you are away, up to eight hours. Cargo drones, ore tugs, mining claims and fuel depots bring in credits (the same credits as the ring's); comms relays and survey guild seats bring standing; survey probes bring star charts; medevac skiffs bring favours. Star charts and favours also come from the ring: chart tours, sim runs and the viewport; rad flushes, vigils and steam soaks.

Refits raise prices, bring more ships, keep guests waiting longer, make combos work better, and license what you can't build at first: cabin berths, the stout synth, sim rigs, steam tubs, the chapel and the sim league. Broadcast a hail for more ships, host a gala (four favours) to sell everything dearer for a while, or sell a survey for credits. Sell the ring's charter to start over grander, with charters that make everything faster: of each kind of contract, two move with you, and one more for every charter. With ten charters, float a belt consortium for shares that stay for good.

A season is seven nights. The game saves itself as you play. In the menu: Play endless keeps the ring open night after night, with more room and five decks; Build from a Dwellings house fits out the ring after a plan made with watabou's Dwellings (its "Export as JSON"), and Export saves yours in the same format.
