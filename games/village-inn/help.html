The Wheatsheaf

You keep the inn on the village green, where the king's road crosses the drovers' way. At evening the door opens and the realm comes in off the green: farmhands and millers, peddlers with their mules, merchants on horseback, soldiers from the garrison, quarrymen white with dust, wandering bards, pilgrims on their way to the church, scholars from the Academy and, now and then, a royal messenger in a hurry.

  1. By day, build and furnish. Build rooms next to the inn, cell by cell, then furnish them. Everything costs silver. You can lay a different floor in a cell (its finish), turn furniture, and rename the inn.
  2. At evening, the door is open. Guests walk to the nearest place that serves their most pressing need, pay and are served. A guest whose need nobody serves waits, sulks and leaves angry, so build for the guests who are coming.
  3. The kitchen garden. The kegs tap ale and the kitchen range cooks stew from the larder's provisions. The well in the yard gives water for nothing: vegetable patches grow on it, and the bread oven bakes their vegetables into pies, dearer than stew. The herb bed grows lavender and rue for the strewing herbs, and the copper heats water for the tubs.
  4. The stables. Merchants and royal messengers want their horse seen to before themselves: a horse stall (with hay from a hayrick) pays well, but horses make muck, and nobody wants to eat next to it. A stable lad's barrow mucks out round it, strewing herbs and flower tubs sweeten the air, and a wall with a door keeps the smell in the stables. Peddlers' mules only want a drink at the water trough. A vegetable patch grows twice as fast where a little muck reaches it.
  5. Mind the rooms. Noise, warmth, dirt, muck and cheer spread from cell to cell (see Show). Scholars and pilgrims hate noise and muck; everyone minds dirt; most guests like cheer, which lanterns, flowers, banners, the fire pit, bards and music give off. Quarrymen like it warm.
  6. Reviews. Happy guests raise your reputation, which brings more guests and new kinds of them: millers at 5, merchants at 8 (horses), soldiers at 12 (thirsty and bored), quarrymen at 15 (grimy), bards at 20 (bored, and cheerful company), pilgrims at 25 (prayer), scholars at 30 (books) and royal messengers at 40 (a fresh horse, now).
  7. Walls, doors and floors. Guests walk round inner walls, and noise, muck and the rest stop at them; a door lets both through. Stairs from the passage or the common room open the floor above ([ and ] switch floors): the quietest place for the chambers and the quiet room.

Rooms

  • Passage, Common room, Kitchen, Cellar: drink, food and stores; a fire pit and a minstrels' stage in the common room.
  • Guest chambers: shared beds and, with feather beds from the market, a bed to oneself. Hayloft: straw pallets, cheap.
  • Yard and garden: the well, vegetable patches, the herb bed, the water trough, an old oak and, later, a maypole.
  • Stables: horse stalls, the hayrick, the stable lad's barrow and, with the royal post, post horses for the king's messengers.
  • Bathhouse: wooden tubs and a copper for the quarry dust (a washstand in the passage or a chamber does a quick wash).
  • Skittle alley: skittle lanes and a draughts table for the bored. Loud.
  • Quiet room: a prayer corner and pews for the pilgrims, bookcases and reading desks for the scholars, and a cabinet of curiosities dug up in the fields.
  • Brewhouse (opened at the market): a mash tun brews best ale.

Combos

Some furniture works better together (a unit's combos show when you point at it). Distances count diagonals, on the same floor.

  • Fireside: a table within 2 cells of the fire pit. Dearer, happier guests.
  • Fresh from the oven: a table within 2 cells of the bread oven. More filling.
  • Regulars' row: bar stools beside the bar. Faster drinks.
  • Under the colours: a table beside a garrison banner.
  • Sing-song: a bar within 2 cells of a minstrels' stage. Dearer, quicker, renown.
  • Lit prayers: two lanterns or candle stands beside the prayer corner.
  • Full pews: two pews beside the prayer corner, once the market has an alms box.
  • A blessed house: any prayer corner, once the parson has blessed the house.
  • Steam room: a tub beside the copper, where the warmth is 3 or more.
  • Herb bath: a tub beside strewing herbs.
  • Quiet corner: a reading desk with two bookcases beside it, noise under 3.
  • Skittle alley: two skittle lanes side by side. Dearer, merrier, louder.
  • Wagers on the board: draughts tables, once reputation is 40.
  • A well-kept stable: a stall or the post horses beside a stable lad's barrow.
  • Posting house: post horses with a stall and a hayrick within 2 cells.
  • A quiet night: a bed where the noise is under 2. Cosy corner: a bed or pallets beside a flower tub or a lantern.
  • The Archaeological Society: a cabinet of curiosities with a reading desk and a bookcase within 2 cells, once the Society has its charter.
  • Dancing on the green: a minstrels' stage within 2 cells of the maypole.

The market

The market stall (its tab next to Inn) runs on through the day and while you are away (up to eight hours). Stable lads, fiddlers, field hands, a cook's helper, ballad-sellers, almoners, carriers' carts, a brewer at the fairs and the drovers' herds work for the inn, paid from the same purse. Field hands bring vegetables to the inn's stores, and the cook's helper bakes them into pies for the market (the bread oven wants them too). Guests feed it: renown from happy guests, tales from songs and reading, alms from prayers. Its improvements raise prices, bring more guests, keep them waiting longer, open the feather beds, the post horses, the brewhouse, the maypole and the Archaeological Society, and make combos stronger. Renown can't run ahead of the inn: it holds 50, plus 2 for every guest served this run. Pass the inn on for legacy (prices and the market rise with it) and build a grander one: of each kind of hired people, two come with you, and one more for every legacy. Much later, give up your legacy for a royal warrant.

A season is seven evenings. The game saves itself as you play. In the menu: Play endless keeps the inn open evening after evening, with more room and five floors to build; Build from a Dwellings house starts an inn in a house made with watabou's Dwellings (its "Export as JSON"), and Export saves yours in the same format.
